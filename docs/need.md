# Evidence of Need
  - We looked through the apple app store to get a gauge on what kind of music theory apps already existed. After scrolling through the top apps of the search page under "music theory", all the apps were free but came with the subtitle of "in app purchases", meaning many of these apps are "freemium" apps that eventually restrict your learning behind a paywall. This is how many educational apps are, they focus on profit rather than making learning early and accessible. Therefore, there is a need for a free app focused on learning rather than profit, especially music theory considering the cases with many of the current tools available in the app store.

# Interview(s)
  - I (Ella) saw a friend from high school recently who is pursuing a career in musical theater. He attends a theater focused university in New York, and works mainly with other people who are involved with music. In working with vocals, there needs to be a deep understanding of music theory and the way that a cast's vocals can work together or with instrumentals themselves. I asked him what he thought of having a free app to learn music theory and he agreed that it would be beneficial for people like him, because him and many of his peers had the privilege of paying for music lessons throughout their childhood to get the necessary knowledge, but many people are unable to afford that/are not in a situation where they can make that paid time commitment. And since teaching music can be such a time commitment, many teachers are only able to handle so many clients, so there is a limited amount of resources for people looking to learn music theory in a given area.

# Existing App Critique
Waay: Learn Music Theory 
- this is the top nonsponsored search result under music theory in the apple app store. It's free with in-app purchases. The app gives video lessons and then quizzes that test the knowledge you've gone over. It also follows a track where you begin on a subject in that category and must score well on the quizzes to continue to move through the lessons. However, if you want to learn something that isn't melodies you have to pay about $60 a year for a plan to be able to access the rest of the app. It's very much a "freemium" tool. The app could be improved by offering those trials on multiple subjects, since users are not just learning melodies when it comes to music theory, they're likely to want to try their other courses like the ones covering chords and progressions. Giving the free portion as a specific section is unwise, and the paywall being 60 dollars a year for an app is also a downside. The quizzes could also be more engaging.
